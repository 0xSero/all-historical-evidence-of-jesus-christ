## Approach

The dossier keeps three kinds of claim apart and labels each one:

- **Historical confidence.** What the evidence supports, and how strongly.
- **Textual claims.** What a given source actually says.
- **Theological conclusions.** Divine purpose, fulfillment, revelation, salvation.

It also tracks two chronologies throughout:

- **Event chronology.** When Jesus is said to have done something.
- **Document chronology.** When an account was composed, first attested, and copied.

A newly discovered copy is not a newly discovered eyewitness.

The dossier grades evidence as one of five kinds: explicit report, independent corroboration, contextual corroboration, historical inference, or theological interpretation. It assigns no numerical probabilities to theological propositions. The minority view that Jesus never existed is evaluated as a historical proposal.

## Contents

1. **Guide to the research and the world before Jesus.** Scope, central findings, evidence standards, a master chronological spine, Second Temple Judaism, and a controlled comparison with older religions and comparable figures.
2. **Jesus's life.** A chronological catalogue of the canonical narratives: origins, birth and childhood, John the Baptist, the Galilean ministry, the journey to Jerusalem, John's festival itinerary, Passion week, arrest, execution and burial, the Easter narratives, and Jesus acting from heaven in the rest of the New Testament.
3. **The prophecies associated with Jesus.** A critical catalogue that gives each passage's original setting next to its Christian reading. It also covers Jesus's own predictions and the expectations that his earthly life did not straightforwardly fulfill.
4. **The documentary record and the movements that interpreted him.** Paul's letters, the manuscripts, other early Christian witnesses, non-Christian and hostile testimony (Josephus, Tacitus, Pliny, Suetonius), Jewish countertraditions, the apocryphal and Gnostic archive, competing portraits from the 1st to 15th centuries, and Jesus in Islam.
5. **From medieval devotion to the present.** Visions and reform, new histories and new revelations, 20th-century historical method and discoveries, and controversies from 1988 to 2026.
6. **What the evidence can and cannot establish.** The historical core, the resurrection argument at its strongest and the steps it leaves unresolved, archaeology item by item, relics and scientific claims, prediction and probability arguments, and open questions.
7. **Sources and further reading.** Numbered references for the context, canonical, prophecy, and ancient-records sections. Contents entries and bracketed source numbers in the PDF are clickable.

## Central finding (summary)

The strongest historical reconstruction is that Jesus was a Jewish man associated with Nazareth and Galilee. He engaged with the kingdom of God and Israel's scriptures, gathered followers, became known for teaching and for healing or exorcistic activity, and was crucified under Pontius Pilate. His followers proclaimed very early that God had raised him.

The dossier treats the following as **separate propositions** that evidence can bear on independently:

- the historical existence of Jesus
- the truth of each Gospel narrative
- the occurrence of a supernatural resurrection
- the doctrine of the incarnation
