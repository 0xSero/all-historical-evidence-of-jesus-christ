# The Great Isaiah Scroll (1QIsaᵃ)

> c. 2nd century BCE (manuscript). Source record: Israel Museum, Shrine of the Book; Israel Antiquities Authority digital collection.

The Great Isaiah Scroll, catalogued 1QIsaᵃ, is the most complete biblical scroll
from Qumran Cave 1. It contains the whole book of Isaiah and is dated on palaeographic
and radiocarbon grounds to the second century BCE. It is displayed in the Shrine of the
Book at the Israel Museum, and the Israel Antiquities Authority publishes high-resolution
images.

What it establishes: that Isaiah existed as a book, in substantially the form known from
later tradition, centuries before Christianity. It also shows textual variation: the
scroll differs from the Masoretic text in many details, which matters for any argument
that depends on the exact wording of a quoted verse.

What it does not establish: that the Qumran community, or anyone else, read Isaiah as a
prediction of Jesus. The scroll is evidence for the pre-Christian textual history of
scripture. Christian readings of Isaiah (7:14; 53; 61) are interpretation of a text that
already existed, not evidence that the text was written about Jesus.

The scroll is one of about 900 manuscripts from eleven caves, recovered between 1947 and
1956, and published progressively.
