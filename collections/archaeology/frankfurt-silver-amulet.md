# The Frankfurt silver amulet (Nida)

> c. 230–270 CE. Source record: Archaeological Museum Frankfurt; find published 2024.

A small silver foil amulet was found in 2018 in a Roman-period grave at
Frankfurt-Heddernheim, the site of the Roman town of Nida. It was rolled inside a capsule
worn on a cord and could not be unrolled without destroying it, so it was read by
computed tomography and then by micro-X-ray fluorescence.

The published reading (2024) contains a Greek invocation naming Jesus, including
`ἅγιος, ἅγιος, ἅγιος` ("holy, holy, holy") and the formula "in the name of Jesus". The
grave is dated to roughly 230-270 CE on the basis of the associated finds.

What it establishes: evidence of Christian devotion far from the Mediterranean heartland
in the third century. It is among the earliest Christian objects found north of the Alps.

What it does not establish: the beliefs of the deceased beyond the formula, or any link to
the Gospel texts. The reading is scholarly reconstruction from imaging, and the editors
present some words as uncertain.
