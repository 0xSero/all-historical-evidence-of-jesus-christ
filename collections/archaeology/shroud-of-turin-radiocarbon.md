# The Shroud of Turin: radiocarbon and later dating claims

> 1988 radiocarbon; 2022 X-ray proposal. Source record: Nature 337 (1989) 611-615; Heritage 5 (2022) 860-870.

In 1988, three laboratories (Arizona, Oxford, and the Swiss Federal Institute of
Technology) dated samples from the Shroud of Turin by accelerator mass spectrometry. They
reported a calibrated range of about 1260-1390 CE, published in *Nature* in 1989.

The sampling, the cleaning protocol, and the statistical treatment were contested at the
time and remain contested by some researchers. In 2022, Liberato De Caro and colleagues
published a wide-angle X-ray scattering (WAXS) study in *Heritage* arguing for a much
earlier date, roughly the first century. The WAXS method measures cellulose ageing and
depends on assumed environmental history; it has not displaced the radiocarbon result for
most specialists.

Other work has examined whether the 1988 samples were contaminated by repair or by
handling (Freer-Waters and Jull, *npj Heritage Science*, 2021 on textile fragments), and
whether bleaching treatments affected the linen (Barta and Ruiz-Navas, *npj Heritage
Science*).

What it establishes: a linen cloth of the 13th-14th century on the radiocarbon evidence,
bearing an image whose formation is not fully explained. The 1988 measurement is the one
result that has been independently replicated by three laboratories.

What it does not establish: any connection to Jesus. Even a first-century date, if
established, would establish the age of a cloth, not its provenance or the identity of the
person depicted. The dossier treats the shroud as a case study in how dating claims and
their assumptions are argued, not as evidence for the resurrection.
