# The Caiaphas ossuary

> 1st century BCE–1st century CE; found 1990. Source record: Israel Antiquities Authority; Israel Museum.

An ornate limestone ossuary (a bone box) was recovered in 1990 from a burial cave in
the Peace Forest, south of Jerusalem's Old City, during construction work. It carries two
Aramaic inscriptions: `Yehosef bar Qayafa` — "Joseph son of Caiaphas" — and, more
roughly, `Qafa`.

The Israel Antiquities Authority published the find, and the ossuary is now in the
Israel Museum. The burial cave was a family tomb of the first century; the archaeological
report dates its use to that period.

What it establishes: a family of high-priestly rank used the name Caiaphas in Jerusalem
in roughly the period the Gospels assign to the high priest who interrogated Jesus. The
Gospels name him "Caiaphas" (Matthew 26:3, 57; John 11:49; 18:13-14).

What it does not establish: that these bones are those of the Caiaphas of the Gospels, or
anything about the trial narratives. The identification is plausible and widely
reported, not proven. The Gospels themselves give the high priest the name Caiaphas and
Josephus gives a "Joseph, who was called Caiaphas" as high priest c. 18-36 CE, so the
name is independently attested; the box belongs to a family of that name.
